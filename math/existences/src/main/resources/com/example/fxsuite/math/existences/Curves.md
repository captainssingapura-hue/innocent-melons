# Curves

The kinds of curve: each a way of giving one value for each x over a span, the same every time, told by how that
value is worked out from what the curve is given. A closed curve, a circle, is two of them over one parameter. A
curve is nobody's making and of no era; nothing in the world is one, but follows one.

## Curve

One value for each x over a span, the same every time.

## Polynomial

Worked out as a sum of powers of x, each by a number of its own.

## Constant

The same value all along: a floor, a level deck.

## Linear

Changing evenly from one end to the other: a ramp, a plain slope.

## Parabolic

Rising to a crown and falling again, or the other way, its slope changing evenly: an arched deck.

## Cubic

Leaving one end and meeting the other each at a slope it is given.

## Conic

Along a cut through a cone, as far as the cut gives one value for each x.

## CircularArc

Along a circle, of a radius it is given: an arch, the edge of a round plan.

## EllipticArc

Along an ellipse, flatter one way than the other: an elliptic arch.

## Transcendental

Worked out by a function that no polynomial is.

## Sinusoidal

Rising and falling as a wave does, by a sine: a reverse curve from one level to another; two of them, a circle.

## Catenary

Hanging as a chain does from its two ends; turned over, the arch that stands in compression alone.

## Clothoid

Turning more and more evenly as it goes, its curvature growing with its length: a road's transition from straight to
a bend.

## Piecewise

Through points given in order, a piece between each two.

## Stepped

Level from each point to the next, rising or falling at once at each: a flight of steps.

## Folded

Straight from each point to the next, turning at each: a roof's slope folded at its purlins.

## Spline

Through its points smoothly, each piece a polynomial, meeting the next at the same slope.

## MonotoneCubic

Smoothly through its points, never rising past them or falling short of them between: an eave's line, a profile.

## NaturalCubic

Smoothly through its points, bending as little as it can, as a thin lath bent through them does.

## Composite

Curves of other kinds made into one.

## Chained

Curves of other kinds end to end, each from where the one before it ends: a road's straight, its spiral, its arc.
