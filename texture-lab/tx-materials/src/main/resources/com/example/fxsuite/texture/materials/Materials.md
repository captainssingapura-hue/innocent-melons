# Materials

What made things are made of. Whatever is here is already shaded: a material is added only with how it is drawn, so a
thing may be made of any of these and be drawn. A leaf is a material as it is seen: two kinds of brick whose faces are
nothing alike are two leaves, though both are fired clay. Only the materials something has so far been made of and
drawn in are here.

## Matter

What a made thing is made of.

## Metal

Worked from ore: strong for its thickness, and it takes a polish.

## Gold

A soft, heavy metal that does not tarnish.

## Bronze

Copper alloyed with tin: harder than either.

## Titanium

A light, strong metal that does not rust.

## Gunmetal

A bronze with zinc in it, cast for what must bear wear.

## Steel

Iron with a little carbon in it: strong and cheap, drawn into tube and pressed into sheet.

## Masonry

Stone, or what is made to stand in for stone, laid piece on piece to stand.

## CommonRedBrick

Clay shaped in a mould and fired hard and red, laid in thick joints of pale mortar.

## ChineseGreyBrick

Clay fired and then starved of air as it cools, so it comes out grey: longer and flatter than the red, and laid in
fine joints of white lime.

## Adobe

Earth and straw shaped in a mould and dried in the sun, not fired.

## Rubble

Stones as they are found or broken, fitted together unshaped.

## DressedStone

Stone cut square, to lie in courses.

## Concrete

Cement, sand and stone poured wet and set hard, cast in slabs with a joint between each.

## Asphalt

Stones bound with tar and rolled flat, as a road is paved: dark grey, worn paler in patches, speckled with its
stones.

## Synthetic

Made by a process, not found and not grown.

## Enamel

A hard coat baked onto what it covers.

## Rubber

A tough, springy stuff that gives and comes back.

## RoadPaint

The paint a road's lines are painted in: white and matt, worn greyer in patches, with no clear coat.

## GlossPaint

Colour sprayed onto sheet and sealed under a clear, hard lacquer: whatever colour the thing was to be.

## MetallicPaint

Colour with fine flakes of metal lying in it, sprayed onto sheet and sealed under a clear, hard lacquer: it shines
wide and soft where gloss paint only glints, and glitters seen close.

## Glass

Sand melted and cooled clear, rolled into sheets.

## Luminous

What gives light of its own.

## LitGlass

Glass with a light behind it.

## Flame

Gas, burning.

## LitPaper

Paper stretched over a frame, a lantern, with a light inside it: it glows in its own colour, which is the
thing's, told with each.

## Grown

What grew, and was not made.

## Rind

The tough outer skin of a fruit.

## Timber

Wood sawn and worked, its grain running along it, under a coat of varnish that shines a little: a boat's posts,
rails and benches.

## Bark

The outer skin of a tree's trunk and boughs, as each kind of tree grows its own.

## OakBark

An oak's bark: grey-brown and rough, its narrow furrows running along the wood, with weather-stains and spots of
lichen.

## WalnutBark

A walnut's bark: grey ridges standing as long narrow plates between dark furrows that run along the wood, parting
and meeting again in diamonds.

## BirchBark

A white birch's bark: chalky white and smooth, with grey bands where its papery skin peels, short dark dashes
lying across the wood, and black patches low on an old trunk.

## PoplarBark

A poplar's bark: pale grey with a cast of green and smooth, marked with small dark flecks, and broken into dark
rough ridges low on an old trunk.

## WillowBark

A weeping willow's bark: grey-brown and deeply furrowed on its trunk and boughs, and golden where its wood is young
and slender, as its hanging twigs are.

## PlaneBark

A plane tree's bark: smooth and shed in flakes, a patchwork of pale cream, olive and grey-brown, and olive where its
wood is young and slender.

## Liquid

What flows, and lies level where it is held.

## Water

What rain is, lying still where the ground or a rim holds it.

## Ground

What the land is covered with, where nothing is built on it.

## Turf

Grass kept short, by grazing or by mowing, and the mat of roots it grows in.

## Topsoil

Bare earth: the dark upper soil things grow in, with nothing growing on it.

## Sand

Rock worn to grains, loose and dry.

## Gravel

Small stones, loose, as a river leaves them or a track is laid with them.

## Bedrock

Rock as it lies in the ground, where the soil has gone from it.

## Mud

Earth and water together, soft, at a water's edge or in a hollow.

## Bodily

What a human body is made of.

## Outward

What is seen of a person from outside.

## Skin

What covers a body: its colour is the person's own.

## Lip

The soft red edge of the mouth.

## Nail

The hard plate at the end of a finger or a toe.

## Hair

What grows from the skin in strands, on the head, the brow and the chin: its colour is the person's own.

## Sclera

The white of the eye.

## Iris

The coloured ring of the eye, round its dark middle: its colour is the person's own.

## Cornea

The clear skin over the front of the eye, seen through and wet.

## ToothEnamel

The hard white coat of a tooth.

## Inward

What is under the skin, seen where a body is shown opened.

## Bone

What a body's frame is made of, as it is when laid bare.

## Cartilage

The smooth firm stuff on the ends of bones and in the nose and the ear.

## Muscle

The red flesh that pulls, in fibres side by side.

## Tendon

The pale cord that ties a muscle to a bone, and the like bands that tie bone to bone.

## Fat

The soft yellow stuff under the skin, in small lobes.
