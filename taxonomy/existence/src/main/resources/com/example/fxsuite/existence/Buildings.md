# Buildings

The kinds of building, by what each is for. A kind says what the building does for those who use it, never how it is
built: a shophouse is a shophouse in timber or in brick.

## Building

A thing built to stand: for people to be in, or to carry, bar or mark.

## Residence

Built to be lived in.

## House

One household's home, standing on its own or in a row.

## Farmhouse

A home in the country, its sheds and yard with it.

## CourtyardHouse

A household's halls and rooms round a courtyard, behind a gate.

## HomesTower

Many homes stacked, reached by lifts.

## Commerce

Built to trade or to work in.

## ShopHouse

A shop on the street with a home over it or behind it.

## WineHouse

Drink and meals sold, its rooms over a hall, its front made to be seen.

## TeaHouse

Tea and food sold at tables, open to the street.

## Stall

A seller's stand, light and put up for the day.

## Warehouse

Goods kept, by a road or a quay.

## FestiveGateway

A decorated frame before a wine house's door, announcing it.

## OfficeTower

Offices stacked, reached by lifts from a lobby.

## HotelTower

A hotel's rooms stacked over its lobby and its halls.

## MixedTower

A tower of more than one use, stacked: offices under homes or a hotel.

## Sacred

Built to worship in.

## Temple

A hall or halls for rites, on a platform, round a court.

## Church

A hall for a congregation, its tower or spire with it.

## Civic

Built for a town's own business and pleasure.

## Pavilion

An open hall to stand or sit under.

## Courthouse

Courtrooms and their offices.

## Infrastructure

Built to carry or to land.

## Bridge

Carries a way over water or over another way.

## Quay

A bank made to moor at and to load from.

## Fortification

Built to keep out.

## GateTower

A gate through a wall, and the tower over it.

## CityWall

A wall round a town, walked along its top.
