# Terrains

The base layer: the land as it lies before anything is on it or cut into it. Every other layer is laid over it and
none changes it. A kind says what the ground is like, never where it is.

## Terrain

The land as it lies: its shape, and what naturally covers it.

## Landform

The shape of the ground.

## Flat

Ground level enough to walk across and to build on without first levelling it.

## Plain

Wide, level ground, rising and falling only a little.

## Floodplain

A plain beside a river, built up by its floods: level, low, its soil deep.

## Plateau

Level ground high above the land round it, falling away at its edges.

## Raised

Ground rising above what is round it.

## Hill

Ground rising to a top, its sides sloping down all round.

## Ridge

A long rise, its top running along it, falling away either side.

## Dune

A rise of sand heaped by the wind.

## Sunken

Ground lying below what is round it.

## Valley

Low ground between rises, running along their length, often with water along its floor.

## Hollow

A small low place, closed all round.

## Gorge

A deep, narrow valley, its sides steep.

## Sloped

Ground falling steadily one way.

## Slope

Ground falling one way, walked up or down.

## Cliff

Ground falling too steeply to climb: a face of rock or earth.

## Cover

What naturally lies on the ground, where nothing has been laid or grown on it by people.

## Bare

Ground with nothing growing on it.

## Earth

Bare soil.

## Sand

Loose grains, dry or wet.

## Clay

Fine, heavy soil, slick when wet.

## Rock

Bare stone at the surface.

## Grown

Ground with something growing on it of itself.

## Grass

Short grass, close over the ground.

## Meadow

Long grass and flowers, uncut.

## Moss

A low, soft cover on damp ground and stone.

## Scrub

Low bushes, close together.

## Forest

Trees standing close, their crowns meeting overhead, the ground under them shaded.
