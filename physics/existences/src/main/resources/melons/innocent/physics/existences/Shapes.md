# Shapes

The shapes a thing that keeps its shape may have, as physics tells them apart: by how each meets what it stands on,
and so how it rests and how it goes when it is no longer held. A shape here is a concept and not a mesh; what is drawn
of a thing may be far finer than the shape it is reckoned as.

## Shape

The shape of a thing that keeps its shape, as it meets what it stands on.

## Faceted

Flat faces only, meeting at edges. It rests on a face and tips over an edge, and never rolls.

## Box

Six flat faces square to each other: a crate, a brick, a beam, a slab.

## Plate

A box so thin one way that it lies flat or falls flat, and stands on an edge only until it is touched: a door's leaf,
a pane, a plank, a sign.

## Prism

An outline stood up, the same all the way up it, flat at top and bottom: a wedge, a roof's block, a building seen
from outside.

## Lump

Flat faces every way, wherever its corners make them, with no dent in it: a rock, a piece of rubble.

## Rounded

No flat face anywhere. It touches what it stands on at one spot, and rolls whichever way the ground falls.

## Ball

As far from its middle every way, so it lies the same however it is turned: a ball, a boulder, a round fruit.

## Ellipsoid

Round every way but longer one way than another: a watermelon, an egg, a pebble. It lies on its side and rolls with
a wobble, and does not stay on its end.

## Turned

Round one way, about a line through it. Lying on its round side it rolls across that line and not along it.

## Drum

Round about its line and flat at both ends: a barrel, a log, a pipe, a can, a wheel. On its side it rolls; on an end
it stands as a box does and tips over its rim.

## Cone

Round about its line and narrowing along it, to a point or to a smaller end: a traffic cone, a bottle, a tapering
trunk. On its base it stands; on its side it rolls round in a circle about its narrow end.

## Capsule

Round about its line with a round cap at each end, so it has no flat face at all: a limb, or what stands in for a
body. It lies on its side and rolls, and never stands on an end.

## Ring

Round about its line with a hole through its middle: a tyre, a hoop. On its tread it rolls as a wheel does until it
falls over; on its side it lies flat.

## Halved

A round shape cut flat. It stands on the cut, and set on its round side it rocks.

## Dome

Half of a ball or of an ellipsoid: the half melon a melon house is, a bowl turned over.

## Compound

Shapes of other kinds made into one that keeps its shape.

## Joined

Several shapes fixed to each other, standing on whichever of them reach down: a table on its legs, a chair, a parked
car on its wheels. It rests on its feet and tips over the line between two of them.

## Fixed

What never moves, and only holds others up. It may be of any shape, since nothing is asked of it but where its top
is.

## Level

Flat ground at one height. Nothing set on it rolls of itself.

## Slope

Flat ground tilted one way: a ramp, a bank, a leaning deck. What rolls, rolls down it, and what does not either
stays or slides.

## Land

Ground whose height changes from place to place: a hillside, a country's terrain.

## Mesh

Any fixed shape at all, given by its faces: a sculpture, a building too odd for an outline.
