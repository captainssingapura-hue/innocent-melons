# Movers

Whatever goes over a surface, told apart by how it meets the surface and not by what it is. Each way of meeting a
surface wants other things told of the surface; a mover asks them of whatever it is on.

## Mover

Whatever goes over a surface, as it meets that surface.

## Stepping

By footfalls. It wants its footing told, the height of a step, and the gap it can cross; it takes a stair, and it
pushes itself along.

## Walker

One that goes on two legs: a person, the avatar, a bot.

## Beast

One that goes on four legs or more.

## Wheeled

By patches that roll under it. It wants told that the surface goes on unbroken, how steep it is, and how well it
grips; a stair stops it.

## Car

A wheeled mover on four wheels or more that drives some of them.

## Motorcycle

A wheeled mover on two wheels in a line, driven, which leans to turn.

## Wagon

A wheeled mover with no drive of its own: it goes as it is pulled, pushed or let roll.

## Tracked

By a long belt laid under it. It wants told how steep the surface is and how soft; it crosses what a wheel sinks in,
and pushes itself along.

## Crawler

A tracked mover: a digger, a tank.

## Sliding

By dragging over the surface. It wants told how the surface grips and how steep it is, and goes only as it is pushed.

## Sled

A sliding mover made to slide, on runners or a smooth underside.

## Dragged

A thing with corners pushed or pulled along on a face: a crate shoved across a floor, or let go on a ramp.

## Rolling

By the whole of itself turning. It wants told how steep the surface is and how it resists rolling, and goes only as
it is pushed.

## Roller

A round thing rolling: a melon, a ball, a barrel on its side.

## Floating

By pushing a liquid aside. It wants told the liquid's level and its depth.

## Boat

A floating mover that drives itself, by a screw, oars or sail.

## Raft

A floating mover with no drive: it goes as the water and the wind take it.
